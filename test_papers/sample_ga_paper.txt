Title: Optimization of Multi-Objective Functions Using Genetic Algorithms

Abstract:
We present a novel genetic algorithm with tournament selection to optimize the multi-objective fitness function f(x) = w1*accuracy + w2*complexity. The input parameter space consisted of 50 decision variables bounded between [0,1]. Population size was set to 100 with crossover probability 0.8 and mutation rate 0.02.

Introduction:
Genetic algorithms are specialized adaptive search algorithms that mimic natural evolution. In this work, we employ real-coded chromosomes with uniform crossover and Gaussian mutation. Performance was evaluated using time-to-collision (TTC) as the primary fitness metric, with constraints ensuring vehicle safety parameters remained within acceptable bounds.

Methodology:
The evolutionary algorithm employed real-coded chromosomes with the following parameters:
- Population size: 100 individuals
- Crossover rate: 0.8 (uniform crossover)
- Mutation rate: 0.02 (Gaussian mutation with σ=0.1)
- Selection: Tournament selection with tournament size k=3
- Generations: 500

The fitness function combined multiple objectives:
f(x) = 0.6*accuracy(x) + 0.3*efficiency(x) + 0.1*robustness(x)

Where accuracy measures the prediction error, efficiency captures computational cost, and robustness evaluates performance under uncertainty.

Results:
Our genetic algorithm converged to optimal solutions within 250 generations on average. The best individual achieved a fitness score of 0.892, representing 89% accuracy with minimal computational overhead.

Conclusion:
The proposed genetic algorithm with adaptive mutation rates successfully optimized complex multi-objective functions in autonomous vehicle control scenarios.
